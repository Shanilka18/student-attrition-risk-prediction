# Data Card: Synthetic Environmental & Financial Survey

## Dataset identity

- **Name:** EF synthetic sample survey, EF-04
- **Version/source:** Configuration-driven generator in `src/data_gen/sample_survey.py`
- **Configured source:** `sample` in `config/project.yaml`; no real student data is used
- **Population represented:** Fictional cross-sectional student respondents with six configured latent types
- **Language/context:** Questionnaire answers follow the configured Environmental & Financial questionnaire labels; income bands use LKR
- **Owner of generation parameters:** `sample_data` in `config/project.yaml`

## Files and intended use

| File | Rows | Use |

Use the data only to test ingestion, feature construction, profiles, model
evaluation code, plots, and UI behavior. The upload files intentionally omit
outcomes. The hidden file contains synthetic type and latent-factor truth; it
must remain separate from predictors and ordinary model-training workflows.
The six EDA plots are listed in `docs/figures/INDEX.md`.

## Generation and parameters

The seed is 42. The full/small/upload sizes are 1,200/300/200. Student-type
shares are stable_supported 34%, financially_strained 18%, commute_burdened
14%, digitally_limited 12%, working_student 12%, and multi_pressure 10%.
Four correlated factors (financial strain, access burden, digital gap, and
home pressure) use the configured type-specific means, a 4 x 4 correlation
matrix, and 0.24 standard deviation, then are clipped to [0, 1].

The 17 questionnaire items are generated from configured item-specific factor
loadings, cut-points or binary rules, pressure order, and response-noise
standard deviations. Fully online response probability is 10%; commute time,
spend, and mode then share structural N/A. Job hours are conditional on having
a part-time job. Full parameter values, including the item-level settings,
are maintained in `config/project.yaml` and summarized in
`docs/03_sample_data.md`.

The score generator uses intercept -2.25; factor weights 0.34, 0.24, 0.22, and
0.20; F x A interaction weight 0.70; latent noise standard deviation 0.80;
score noise standard deviation 8.0; and at-risk threshold 60. Five-fold
stratified CV calibrates signal from 3.00 in 0.10 increments for up to eight
iterations. With seed 42, accepted iteration 2 selected signal 3.10,
prevalence 27.00%, LightGBM ROC-AUC 0.7977, and Logistic ROC-AUC 0.8121.
These are synthetic checks only.

Configured data-quality effects include 5% cost-difficulty non-disclosure,
4% each for acquiescence, midpoint, and extreme response styles, 2%
straight-lining, 2% attention-check failure, 2% random missing item cells,
1% no consent, 0.5% duplicates, and 0.3% typo/formatting changes. Messy
uploads additionally contain 3% blank cells, invalid values in 1% of rows,
alternate accepted headers, and one unknown column. Synthetic IDs use a
`P-` prefix and five digits; timestamps start at the configured date and
advance seven minutes per row. Full details are in the configuration and the
sample-data methodology.

## Collection and processing

There was no collection process, participant recruitment, consent event, or
human-subject observation. All records are generated by code. The generator
writes Google-Forms-style raw headers, text answer labels, synthetic timestamp
and identifier fields, and outcome columns. The clean upload consists only of
the 17 canonical predictor columns. The messy upload intentionally changes
some headers and values to exercise preprocessing.

## Intended users and prohibited uses

Intended users are developers and researchers validating data processing,
methodology, dashboards, and interfaces before approved survey data exists.
Do not use these records to:

- infer facts about real students, institutions, or any demographic group;
- claim predictive performance, calibration, external validity, or fairness
  for real students;
- make or recommend individual student decisions;
- estimate real prevalence, income distribution, or causal impact;
- treat hidden type/factor values as observed student attributes.

## Sensitive attributes and privacy

The synthetic questionnaire includes household income bands and money-related
responses, which are sensitive in a real survey. These values are not real
financial details. Synthetic IDs and timestamps are not anonymization
procedures for eventual real collection. For real data, follow approved
consent, access control, retention, de-identification, aggregation, and
small-group suppression requirements; do not copy this sample's handling as a
privacy guarantee.

## Known limitations and risks

- Latent types and all answer/outcome relationships are authored assumptions.
- The label is a generated intention score, not observed withdrawal; for a
  real questionnaire it remains a self-reported intention proxy.
- There is no time dimension, repeated observation, intervention, or causal
  interpretation.
- Calibration is performed on data generated from the same latent design,
  which makes it a software/method check rather than independent validation.
- The fixed response-quality mechanisms do not cover the full range of
  real-world response behaviors or missing-data mechanisms.
- Income sensitivity and possible response bias cannot be assessed with
  synthetic data.
- The synthetic metrics do not establish group-wise fairness.
- Generated profile recovery against the hidden truth tests implementation
  behavior but cannot show that discovered profiles exist in a real cohort.

## Maintenance

Regenerate with `python -m efi.cli make-sample-data`. Keep all numeric
generation and quality settings in `config/project.yaml` under `sample_data`.
When real survey data becomes available, use configuration-driven source
selection and keep it outside this synthetic sample card and repository
fixtures. Review the methodology and figures index whenever generator
parameters or outputs change.
