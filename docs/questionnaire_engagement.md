You are invited to take part in an undergraduate research study conducted at the Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology (SLIIT). ## 6. Conceptual Grounding for the Seven Indicators

### Indicator 1: Resource Access (`resource_access`)
- **What it measures**: The frequency (days per week) with which a student accesses online course repositories, lecture slides, and digital reading materials.
- **Why it matters for attrition**: Early disengagement from primary learning resources is one of the earliest visible warning signs of academic detachment. Students who stop accessing course materials typically accumulate knowledge deficits that become unrecoverable by mid-semester.
- **Related construct**: *Behavioral Engagement / Digital Learning Resource Utilization* (citation to be added by author).

### Indicator 2: Independent Study Time (`study_time`)
- **What it measures**: Total weekly volume of independent, self-directed academic effort outside of timetabled class contact hours.
- **Why it matters for attrition**: Credit-bearing higher education modules require 2–3 hours of independent study per contact hour. A persistent drop in study hours indicates either competing external commitments (work/family) or psychological withdrawal, both strongly linked to dropout.
- **Related construct**: *Academic Effort Allocation / Time-on-Task* (citation to be added by author).

### Indicator 3: Review of Prior Material (`review_time`)
- **What it measures**: Deliberate time spent revisiting earlier content to consolidate understanding and retain prerequisite concepts.
- **Why it matters for attrition**: Students at high attrition risk frequently fall into a passive "catch-up" trap where past difficult material is skipped. Lack of review time signals superficial learning and cognitive overload, preceding exam failure.
- **Related construct**: *Cognitive Engagement / Self-Regulated Review Strategies* (citation to be added by author).

### Indicator 4: Quiz and Practice Engagement (`quiz_practice`)
- **What it measures**: Voluntary or required participation in practice problem sets, diagnostic quizzes, and formative exercises.
- **Why it matters for attrition**: Formative quiz engagement provides self-feedback on mastery. Students who avoid formative practice often do so due to low academic self-efficacy or fear of failure, leading to disengagement before summative evaluations.
- **Related construct**: *Retrieval Practice / Formative Self-Assessment* (citation to be added by author).

### Indicator 5: Assignment Engagement & Punctuality (`assignment_engagement`)
- **What it measures**: The student's conscientiousness in starting coursework promptly and meeting submission deadlines.
- **Why it matters for attrition**: Assignment non-submission or chronic late submission is the single highest proximal precursor to modular failure and formal withdrawal.
- **Related construct**: *Academic Compliance / Conscientious Task Execution* (citation to be added by author).

### Indicator 6: Study Schedule Regularity (`study_consistency`)
- **What it measures**: The regularity, predictability, and temporal stability of study habits across the weeks of a period.
- **Why it matters for attrition**: Chaotic, erratic study habits (binge studying or last-minute cramming) result in burnout, high stress, and volatile grades. Consistent pacing is a protective factor against dropout.
- **Related construct**: *Self-Regulated Learning / Temporal Consistency* (citation to be added by author).

### Indicator 7: Interactive Class Participation (`participation`)
- **What it measures**: Active two-way interaction in lectures, tutorials, lab sessions, and academic peer forums.
- **Why it matters for attrition**: Social and academic isolation is a primary driver of student departure according to established attrition theories. Students who never participate feel disconnected from the academic community.
- **Related construct**: *Social-Behavioral Engagement / Classroom Interactivity* (citation to be added by author).
